01 — Context

What is watsonx.data?

watsonx.data is IBM's open data lakehouse — part of the watsonx family alongside watsonx.ai and watsonx.gov, under Data & AI. It's built to scale workloads and analytics across all your data, wherever it lives.

In plain terms

A data lake stores raw, diverse data cheaply. A data warehouse keeps structured data fast and report-ready. A lakehouse is the hybrid — the flexibility of a lake with the performance of a warehouse, so teams can query data where it sits.

It lives in IBM's Hybrid Data Management portfolio, and my focus there was product-led growth — the experience a prospect has when they first try the product, on their own, without a sales call.

02 — The user

Meet Sue, a data engineer

Data engineers make sure data is accessible to the right people in the right place — moving data to a central location, providing high-quality transformed data for business use, and keeping governance intact along the way.

People like Sue need somewhere to actually work with all that data — a lake, a warehouse, or a lakehouse. watsonx.data is meant to be that place. But first, she has to get in the door.

03 — The problem

Getting in the door was the wall

Before Sue could do anything useful, simply getting watsonx.data provisioned was a slog. The path wound through the marketing site, account creation, the IBM Cloud console, a catalog search, a plan picker, and a resource list — then ended in a long wait.

  1. Start on the watsonx.data marketing page, click "Start a free trial."
  2. Log in or create an IBMid.
  3. Land in the IBM Cloud console and "create a resource."
  4. Search the IBM Cloud catalog for watsonx.data.
  5. Select the Lite plan and kick off provisioning.
  6. Hunt through the resource list — expanding "Databases" — to find the instance.
  7. Open watsonx.data and step through a configuration wizard.
  8. Wait — provisioning could take up to 15 minutes, watching a progress bar.

All told: roughly 10 screens and 9 clicks before the product did anything, capped by a 15-minute provisioning wait staring at a blank loading screen. For a self-serve trial, that's where prospects quietly give up.

04 — The goal

Our problem statement

The problem
"There are too many screens to provision watsonx.data. Simplify the path and cut provisioning from 15 minutes to 1."
05 — The solution

What changed in the to-be

The redesign collapsed the path and removed the dead time — and where waiting was unavoidable, it turned that wait into something useful.

01

One provisioning page, with a use case

Instead of scattering setup across the console and catalog, a single provisioning page lets the prospect pick a use case (like data engineering) that bundles the right engines for them. Choosing the use case up front is the key lever that cuts the bulk of the provisioning time.

02

Consistent across watsonx

The same provisioning page is shared by watsonx.ai and watsonx.gov, so the experience is consistent across the whole watsonx family — and a prospect who knows one knows them all.

03

No more blank-screen wait

When provisioning does take a moment, an educational "preparing your journey" screen teaches key parts of the lakehouse while the user waits — turning dead time into onboarding instead of a frozen progress bar.

04

A welcome, not an empty room

Rather than dropping the user into a bare console, a welcome modal offers two doors: take a quick product tour, or jump straight into sample data and start working.

07 — Reflection

Good design is invisible

No user ever needs to see the behind-the-scenes of design. Underneath this simple flow sat detailed user-flow charts to capture the architecture's nuances, careful design handoff to development, and playback to leadership to keep everyone aligned.

At the core, I'm a designer who cares about the humane side of both the process and the result — and about a safe environment for the team doing the work.
